## Key Insights
1. **Seasonal items = higher margins via emotional buying**: People don't comparison-shop seasonal products; they buy the "feeling of the season." This removes price sensitivity and allows 52-67% margins.
2. **Six proven seasonal items with real P&L**:
   - Alani Witch's Brew (Halloween): $2.15 cost → $4.49 sell, 52% margin, $2.34 profit
   - Starbucks Pumpkin Spice Frappuccino (Fall): $2.76 cost → $6.99 sell, 60% margin, $4.23 profit
   - Firecracker popsicles (Summer): $1.32 cost → $3.99 sell, 67% margin, $2.67 profit
   - Christmas theme Coke bottles: $1.57 cost → $3.99 sell, ~61% margin, $2.42 profit
   - Celsius Pina Colada (Summer): $1.92 cost → $4.49 sell, ~57% margin, $2.57 profit
   - Starbucks Peppermint Mocha Frappuccino (Winter): $2.76 cost → $6.99 sell, 60% margin, $4.23 profit
3. **Universal timing advantage**: Seasonal demand hits all location types simultaneously (gyms, warehouses, apartments, hospitals). No need to customize by location type.
4. **Layer, don't replace**: Seasonal items sit on top of the core planogram. Swap in during relevant weeks, swap out when the season ends.
5. **Perceived novelty = pure profit**: Christmas Coke is the exact same product with different packaging. Customers pay premium for the seasonal experience.
6. **Kande takeaway**: Build a seasonal rotation calendar for all locations. Prioritize freezer-capable machines for summer popsicle programs. The 60%+ margins on seasonal beverages (especially Starbucks Frappuccinos at $4.23 profit/bottle) justify dedicated shelf space. Train stockers on swap schedules so seasonal items don't sit past their window.
